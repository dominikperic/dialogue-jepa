# Candidate good views: research protocol (design only)

**Status:** No JEPA has been implemented or trained; no embeddings or alignment scores exist. This is a falsifiable plan, not a claim that a dialogue-specific Platonic representation exists. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis motivates a test of representational convergence but also discusses limitations and counterexamples.[1] Image JEPA demonstrates latent prediction in images, not its success for speech or dialogue.[2]

## 1. Research questions and operational hypothesis

1. After **independent** audio-only and text-only predictive training, can frozen turn/context representations be aligned using a map fit on *separate* paired anchors, and retrieve their partners on unseen dialogues better than pretrained, untrained, identity-map and shuffled-anchor controls?
2. Does review-supported same-utterance pairing yield more robust alignment than controlled different-turn, same-dialogue pairing, with different-dialogue pairing as a mismatched control?
3. Do either aligned or unimodal representations carry information about *future dialogue-state changes* beyond lexical overlap, trivial state persistence and strong pretrained/unimodal/fusion baselines?
4. Which results survive new dialogues, domains, recording conditions and—only if independently verified speaker IDs permit—held-out speakers?

A positive retrieval result alone would support shared *recoverable information*, not a unique universal latent space, causal state model, or understanding of the conversation.

## 2. What is the proposed shared latent variable?

Let a turn at index `t` have acoustic observation `A_t`, transcript `T_t`, and causal context `C_<t` (only preceding turns). Define the **candidate shared conversational state** `Z_t^shared` operationally as the turn's speaker role, recoverable linguistic content (including intent, entities and negation), and the context-dependent dialogue acts, goals, and slot/state changes that *both* modalities can support from `A_t` or `T_t` plus their respective past. This is a **testable information target, not an observed dataset field or a commitment to one geometric coordinate system**. The corresponding audio and text estimates need not be identical: a future representation may include private audio information `U_t^audio` (prosody, timing, pauses, accent, speaker characteristics, overlap/noise) and private text information `U_t^text` (orthography, punctuation, normalized spellings). A transcript can omit or misstate acoustic evidence; audio does not fix the spelling of a name without external knowledge. Model outputs should not be forced to discard useful private information merely to maximize shared similarity.

Operational evidence for `Z_t^shared` requires **both** (a) controlled, held-out cross-modal retrieval/geometry after fitting a post-training map and (b) an improvement or preserved utility on dialogue-act/state probes relative to strong lexical and acoustic shortcuts. Stratify retrieval by same topic or similar wording but different state, and test state prediction after matching lexical content, so success limited to word identity is visible. Separate a probe's annotation coverage from the learned state: dialogue-state/act labels are *evaluation targets*, never inputs to the unimodal JEPA objective. The SpokenWOZ paper describes speech–text task-oriented conversations and cross-turn/reasoning-slot challenges; it does not establish that either modality fully determines an abstract state.[3]

## 3. Eligibility before view construction

Assign **separate** quality fields for source provenance, WAV presence, time-window bounds, channel/role evidence, transcript review, and timestamp overlap. A bound check or activity ratio is not human validation of words or speakers. Only review-supported transcript/audio agreement **and** independently checked timing/channel selection enter the *initial high-confidence* same-turn anchor pool; otherwise quarantine pending review. The current six-clip pilot contains five user-reported transcript passes and one unresolved proper noun (`MUL0006`, turn 0); those five passes are **not** five automatically qualified anchor pairs. The initial high-confidence subset excludes `MUL0006` turn 0 until its name is resolved. `MUL0008` turn 26 passed transcript review but retains an independent *timestamp-derived* overlap flag; actual overlapping speech was not separately confirmed. Keep it out of the unambiguous single-speaker anchor pool pending review. Do not correct original annotations or infer that all 216 in-bounds windows are transcript-verified. See [audio validation](audio_validation.md) and the preserved git-ignored `data/processed/spokenwoz/review_clips/review_manifest.jsonl`.

The current five locally obtained WAVs and six reviewed clips are a **feasibility pilot**, not a sufficiently sized or speaker-verified training/evaluation corpus. Nothing in this protocol triggers more downloads. Fix a legally permissible, larger sampling plan and verification budget *before* implementation.

## 4. Candidate views and controls

For a common eligible anchor turn `t`, construct the following *post-training comparison* conditions. Keep the same anchor population, embedding extraction policy, number of pairs, candidate-pool size, and split for fair comparisons. Record exactly which human and automated checks each pair passed. Conditions are experimental manipulations, **not proven categories of theoretically “good” or “bad” views**.

| Condition | Pair construction | Control and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A: candidate good view** | `A_t` from the verified role channel and annotated interval, with `T_t` from the same utterance; attach strictly preceding context only where defined. | Review timing, audible text, role channel, and overlap separately. This pair has the strongest *candidate* shared linguistic event, not guaranteed perfect equality of content or state. |
| **B: weak view** | `A_t` with `T_u`, `u ≠ t`, from the **same dialogue**. Stratify turn distance (immediately adjacent versus more distant), signed temporal direction, same/opposite role and local dialogue context. | Use fixed, recorded distance/context strata; exclude duplicate wording and uncertain/missing intervals or report them separately. Same-dialogue topic, speaker and state can be shared, so these are weaker *turn correspondence* anchors, not necessarily semantically false pairs. No future `T_u` is available as context to a causal predictor. |
| **C: mismatched view** | `A_t` with `T_v` from a **different, disjoint dialogue** within the same data partition. | Where observed, match role, domain/topic, duration/word-count bins, transcript length, source/revision and recording-quality bins; do not match by unavailable speaker identity. Report matchable strata, unmatched variables and false negatives (two dialogues may describe the same goal). Also compare random mismatches to harder matched mismatches. |

**Disentangle view quality from supervision amount:** in the primary independent-learning experiment none of A/B/C pairs train either encoder. Use A, B and C to fit **separate frozen post-hoc maps on corresponding alignment-fit anchors**, then evaluate each on the *same untouched A test pairs*; report the B/C map's noise, geometry and test retrieval against A. A secondary fixed-map analysis may score A/B/C on held-out matched candidates, but define the positive for each metric explicitly: B/C do **not** become “true” same-turn positives by relabeling. A shuffled-anchor null, identity/no-fit map, and same-topic/different-dialogue negatives distinguish map fitting from pre-existing similarity. If condition B/C varies dialogue, role, number of samples or pool difficulty, no clean conclusion about view quality follows.

## 5. Experiment 1 — independent training, then alignment

1. Assign entire dialogue IDs to disjoint **unimodal encoder-train**, **development/model-selection**, **paired alignment-fit**, and **untouched evaluation** partitions before creating turn windows. Respect official test isolation; no slices of a dialogue cross partitions. If the available corpus cannot support these partitions and meaningful retrieval pools, stop at the feasibility stage. A verified speaker-disjoint study requires actual speaker identities; absent those, report speaker leakage as unresolved.
2. Train the audio context/target encoders and predictor **only on audio**, and the text equivalents **only on text**. No common learned weights, cross-modal losses, simultaneous paired batches, teacher distillation across modalities, or shared label supervision. Predict a representation of later *within-modality* turns from causal preceding turns. Audit whether any pretrained starting model already used audio–text paired supervision; if so, label that baseline as cross-modal-pretrained rather than independent evidence. Compare suitable scratch controls and strong pretrained audio/text representations **without JEPA training**. Select objectives, encoders, stopping points and non-collapse checks on train/development only.
3. Freeze both encoders/predictors. Extract one specified representation per turn/context; fix normalization, dimensionality and pooling using permitted data. Use only the **alignment-fit** A/B/C anchors to fit a declared post-hoc transformation (e.g. centered orthogonal Procrustes where dimensions permit); no gradients to either encoder. Do not learn an alignment map, whitening, temperature or thresholds on untouched evaluation dialogues. Paired supervision is permissible **only here and in evaluation**, not during independent encoder training.
4. Evaluate **bidirectional** audio→text and text→audio retrieval on paired turns from unseen dialogues. Keep all candidates within the designated evaluation partition; publish candidate pool size/composition, whether other turns from the same dialogue are present, repeated utterances, tie handling and exclusion rules. Compare both no-map and fit-map results to shuffled-anchor maps, random/untrained encoders, pretrained-only representations and within-modality baselines. Report raw and fitted results separately: a map fitted on paired examples is *supervised alignment*, not zero-shot convergence.

## 6. Experiment 2 — causally defined future dialogue-state prediction

The present turn manifest has state snapshots on **system turns**, while user-turn state fields are empty; do not mistake an empty user field for an unchanged state. Number the annotated system turns in each dialogue `s_0, s_1, …`. At the end of system turn `s_k`, use only audio **or** text for turns through `s_k` to predict whether the next annotated system snapshot `S_{k+1}` will change relative to `S_k`. **Primary target:** binary `y_k = 1` if any defined, normalized domain/slot value changes between consecutive system snapshots; otherwise `0`. The horizon is **one subsequent system turn** (typically an intervening user utterance), not one second or an arbitrary future window. Normalize slot schema using training data; do not treat missing snapshots as zeros or silently convert unrecognized values to “unchanged.” Exclude missing endpoints and report support and class balance before any experiment. As a secondary analysis, predict which domain/slot changed or the next dialogue act *only if labels and support suffice*; specify its taxonomy before training.

Compute `y_k` **offline for supervision/evaluation only**. Neither `S_{k+1}`, the intervening user turn, future transcript/audio, future-turn metadata, nor post-`s_k` annotation may enter any model input, pooling, normalization, split assignment or candidate selection. If an explicit `S_k` is supplied as input to a baseline, give every comparable system the same allowed current-state input or report it as a separate information-rich baseline. Keep all windows of a dialogue in one partition. A frozen independent audio-only probe, frozen independent text-only probe, and frozen representations with a learned **post-hoc** alignment are different analyses; a conventionally fused audio+text model is a separately marked supervised baseline, not an independently converged JEPA. Include strong pretrained text-only and audio-only, majority and persistence/current-state, lexical/ASR, simple acoustic, and conventional multimodal baselines with comparable train/evaluation dialogues; document differences in supervised labels, capacity and pretraining provenance. No numerical advantage is assumed.

## 7. Metrics and failure analysis

- **Alignment:** bidirectional Recall@1, Recall@5, mean reciprocal rank (MRR), matched-pair versus controlled mismatched-pair cosine similarity distributions, with candidate counts and ties stated. Recall@5 on pools of fewer than five distinct eligible candidates is uninformative; enlarge the pool or mark it non-comparable. Exclude aliases/duplicate transcriptions from ambiguous one-positive rankings or report multi-positive scoring separately. Report no-map and fitted-map measurements and per-dialogue uncertainty, not just a mean.
- **Prediction:** macro-F1, accuracy and per-class recall for the binary state-change target; report supports, confusion matrix and a majority/persistence baseline. For probabilistic outputs report reliability bins plus a calibration measure (e.g. Brier score/ECE) with fit parameters learned from development data. For any secondary multi-label slot target, specify class handling and micro/macro aggregation separately. Report resampling by *dialogue* and repeated-seed variability, not turn-level IID confidence intervals.
- **Failures:** inspect by role, overlap/ambiguous channel, proper nouns and transcription mismatch, short/long turns, domain, dialogue length/position, context distance, background quality and new speakers if verified. Compare same-lexical-content/different-state and same-state/different-wording examples to detect a lexical-only representation. Audit repeated phrases, speaker/recording-condition cues and domain leakage; distinguish errors in pairing, annotation, state definition, collapse, map fitting and downstream probing. Never upgrade unreviewed turns to “correct” because model similarity is high.

No success threshold, expected effect size or benchmark score is assigned here. Before execution, preregister which metric is primary, splits, sampling sizes, decision criterion and uncertainty treatment without choosing them from test-set outcomes.

## 8. Risks, licensing and implementation gate

The local six-clip review is too small to estimate transcript error rates; transcript uncertainty (`MUL0006` turn 0) and an unresolved actual-overlap question (`MUL0008` turn 26) are **distinct**. Twenty timestamp-overlap flags among the five WAVs require targeted review, and speaker identities remain unverified. Audio-only prosody/pauses/speaker traits may have no faithful text analogue; transcripts can include orthographic conventions absent from audio. Even alignment plus high retrieval might recover only lexical content or trivial recording/domain cues, not predictive conversational state. Unseen speakers cannot be claimed without verified IDs. The official distribution is CC BY-NC 4.0; use must respect attribution and noncommercial terms, and rights to redistribute audio/clips require separate attention.[4]

**Move to implementation only when:** provenance and permissible use are documented; a sufficiently broad, verified paired pool exists beyond the six-clip pilot with uncertainty/overlap handling; complete dialogue-disjoint partitions and candidate pools are fixed; state target schema and missing-label policy are audited; independent pretrained provenance and computational budget are declared; leakage, non-collapse and baseline checks are specified; and evaluation/uncertainty/failure slices are preregistered. If speaker verification or larger samples are unavailable, narrow the claims rather than asserting speaker-level or broad convergence. These gates are procedural, **not arbitrary numeric success thresholds**. Preserve the human review manifest and raw annotations; do not regenerate review clips as a side effect of protocol work.
